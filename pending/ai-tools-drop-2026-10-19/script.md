**NARRATION**

Number two, and my second pick. Indra, from a company called Datalab, released in mid-October. It is an OCR model, which means it reads text out of scans and PDFs. It is tiny, about a million parameters, and Datalab says it reads up to forty-nine pages a second on an ordinary processor, with no graphics card. ## SEGMENT 13 - WHAT LOCAL AI MEANS FOR POPIA (10:20 to 11:15)

**NARRATION**

Two of this week's tools run entirely on your own machine, so here is what that does and does not change. If you are an immigration practice, a clinic or a bookkeeper, the biggest question with AI is usually where your client's information goes. With Llama Windows or Indra running locally, the answer is that it stays on your computer. No overseas server, no vendor storing your client's passport. What it does not remove is your own responsibility. Under POPIA you still need a lawful reason to process the information, you still need to keep it secure, and a stolen laptop is still a breach. So local AI is not a loophole. It is a simpler setup to defend. Encrypt the laptop, keep a short note of what you process and why, and you are in a much better position than pasting client files into a free chatbot.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**

```

Removes: overseas servers, vendor storage

- lawful reason to process
- keep it secure

```
