## SEGMENT 10 - HEMORY (7:59 to 8:58)

**NARRATION**

Number eight took the top spot on Product Hunt on the twenty-sixth, and it needs a warning label. Hemory listens all day on your phone or Apple Watch, splits what it hears into moments with speaker labels, and turns it into searchable memory your AI agent can read over MCP. On audio, it is careful. The company says the original audio stays on your device, what goes to the cloud is discarded after processing, and only text is kept. The problem is not the audio. It is the people. You are recording conversations with others, and Hemory itself says it encourages asking before you record. Text memories live in the cloud with no hosting region and no training statement published, and there was no pricing on the page. My view. Use it for your own voice notes and internal sessions with your team's agreement. Do not take it into a client consultation, especially in health, legal or immigration work.

## SEGMENT 13 - A PRICE WAR YOU CAN USE (10:48 to 11:44)

**NARRATION**

Step back and look at the four big releases side by side. Grok four point seven at two dollars in and six out, the same price as before. GPT-6 Sol at two and ten. Luna at ten cents and fifty. Opus five point five at four and twenty. Every lab either held its price or cut it. For a South African business paying in dollars, that matters more than any single feature. The rand moved from sixteen twenty-five to sixteen forty-three in a week. A price cut on the dollar side absorbs that. So the practical move is simple. Price your AI features on the cheapest model that passes your own test, not the brand you started with. Keep a small set of real examples from your business, run them through each new model when it lands, and re-check every quarter. The winner changes. Your test set should not.
