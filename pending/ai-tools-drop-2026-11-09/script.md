## SEGMENT 9 - FLOOT PAYMENTS (6:44 to 7:42)

**NARRATION**

Tool seven comes with a South African warning label. Floot builds and hosts small apps from a chat in ChatGPT, Claude or Cursor, and on the fourth of November it added payments: subscriptions, once-off payments, deposits, coupons and refunds. You can test the whole checkout free with a test card before anything real happens. Going live needs the Pro plan at twenty-five dollars a month, about four hundred and twelve rand, plus a one percent Floot fee on each payment, plus Stripe's fees. Here is the catch. Live payments run through your own Stripe account, and Stripe lists South Africa as an extended network country, supported through Paystack. One local guide says rand subscriptions work for most cases but have edge cases. So before you build anything, confirm your Stripe account actually connects. If it does not, a South African gateway like Payfast, Yoco or Peach Payments is the safer route for real money.

## SEGMENT 13 - WHICH VIDEO TOOL, IF ANY (10:14 to 11:12)

**NARRATION**

Four of this week's eight tools make video, so here is a simple way to choose. Start with what you already have. If you already film yourself, OpusClip is the one, because it works from your own face and voice, and people trust that more than an avatar. If you have a document but no footage, like a training manual, Synthesia turns it into a video, and it is best kept for staff. If you have nothing but an idea, try a fifteen-second clip in Google Vids first, because it costs nothing extra. And if all you need is a small moving graphic for WhatsApp Status, a ChatGPT GIF is enough. Whatever you pick, two rules. Label AI-made content when you post it, because YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and Facebook all ask you to. And do not pay for two of these at once. Try one for a month, count the hours it saved, and decide in rand.
