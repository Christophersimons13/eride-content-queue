**NARRATION**

Number three. Claude Haiku five point five, released on the seventh of October. Anthropic calls it the cheapest, fastest and most capable small model it has released. It is built for high-volume work: summaries, sorting messages, routing, form-filling and live chat. On the API it costs ten US cents per million input tokens, about one rand sixty-six, and fifty cents per million output tokens, about eight rand twenty-eight. That holds for prompts up to a hundred thousand tokens. Above that the price goes up fivefold, so keep your prompts short. Free users can also pick Haiku five point five in the Claude app. Anthropic is clear that it is best for narrowly scoped tasks, and its new tokenizer uses slightly more tokens per task. Picture a WhatsApp support line that summarises every chat at the end of the day, for a few rand a month.

## SEGMENT 10 - SYNTHID DETECTOR (7:53 to 8:46)

**NARRATION**

Number eight. Google's SynthID Detector, opened to everyone on the seventh of October and available globally in English. You upload an image, a video or an audio file, and it checks for the invisible watermark that Google and its partners put into AI-generated media. Partners include OpenAI and NVIDIA, with Apple coming. It accepts the common formats, JPG, PNG, MP4, MP3 and WAV among them. You need to log in, and you get about ten checks a day. Here is the limitation that matters most. A clean result does not prove a file is real. It only means no watermark was found, and plenty of AI tools use no watermark at all. Still, for a fintech looking at a suspicious proof of payment, or an immigration practice checking a supporting photo, it is a free first check that takes a minute.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
