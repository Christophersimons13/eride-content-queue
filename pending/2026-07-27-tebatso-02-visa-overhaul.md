## The news angle

South Africa is preparing its **largest immigration overhaul in over 20 years**, based on the Revised White Paper on Citizenship, Immigration and Refugee Protection. New visa categories, a merit-based points system, digital application processing, and expanded ETA are all on the table.

**Primary source:** [Getaway Magazine — 7 July 2026](https://www.getaway.co.za/travel-news/south-africa-plans-biggest-visa-overhaul-in-more-than-20-years/)
**Legal commentary:** Wright Rose-Innes — quoted flagging stricter employer compliance from 2026 onwards
**Government basis:** Revised White Paper on Citizenship, Immigration and Refugee Protection

**Why this matters for EMA:** This is a forward-looking positioning piece. Every visa consultant will scramble to understand the new system. EMA already has the framework mapped and can be *the first to explain it plainly*. This builds authority and search relevance for "new SA visa 2026" queries.

## Tebatso script (word-for-word)

> **[0:03 — Tebatso, calm, informed authority tone]**
>
> "South Africa is about to change its entire visa system for the first time in over two decades."
>
> **[0:10 — build the picture]**
>
> "Under the Revised White Paper on Citizenship, Immigration and Refugee Protection, the government is proposing four new visa categories: a **remote worker and digital nomad visa**, a **start-up entrepreneur visa**, a **skilled professional visa**, and a **sports and cultural talent visa**."
>
> **[0:25 — the structural change]**
>
> "Even bigger — a **merit-based, points-based system** for permanent residence and certain visa applications. That means your qualifications, your income, your skills all get scored against a threshold. Score below it, you don't qualify. Score above it, you're in."
>
> **[0:40 — the corporate visa replacement]**
>
> "The old corporate visa system is being replaced with sector-specific work visas. And employers face stricter compliance — you'll need to verify visa validity, monitor expiry dates, and maintain full immigration records. Failure to comply carries criminal penalties."
>
> **[0:55 — the timing reality]**
>
> "The laws still need to pass through Parliament, so current visa rules remain in place. But when this legislation moves — and it will move — the applicants who understand the new system first will be the ones who benefit."
>
> **[1:08 — CTA]**
>
> "At E-Migration Assist, we're already tracking every proposed change in real time. When it becomes law, you'll know exactly which pathway fits your case. Link in bio."
