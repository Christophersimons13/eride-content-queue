## SEGMENT 3 - BUSINESSES IN CHATGPT (1:24 to 2:24)

**NARRATION**

Tool one, and my top pick. Businesses in ChatGPT launched in beta on the tenth of November. Through Business Hub you claim your business, update your details, add a product feed with prices and stock, and see how often you are mentioned and how many people click through to your site. You can verify by phone, business email, your website domain or Shopify, and OpenAI does not mention a fee. Now the catch. OpenAI's help page says local stores and restaurants can currently claim only an existing US listing. So a salon with a shopfront probably has to wait. An online store or a service business with a website has a better chance. After the claim, you pass an identity check with a company called Persona, which can ask for your ID and business registration. I could not confirm that it supports South African records yet. The chatbot that sits on top, called a Business Agent, is US only.

## SEGMENT 13 - GET FOUND BY AI (9:39 to 10:30)

**NARRATION**

One idea ties this week together. More customers ask an AI before they search, and the AI answers from whatever it can find about you. So here is a short checklist. One, make your name, address, phone number and prices identical everywhere: your website, your Google profile and your social pages. Two, if you sell online or have a service website, try to claim your profile in ChatGPT. Three, keep a product or price list on your website as plain text, not only as a photo on Instagram. Four, once a month, ask ChatGPT and Gemini what they know about your business, and write the answer down. If it is wrong, fix the source, not the AI. And five, do not hand any of these tools your client list without checking how they handle personal information.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
