## SEGMENT 3 - ELEVENAGENTS
### 1:40 to 3:20

**NARRATION:**
Number one. ElevenAgents, from ElevenLabs.

On the twenty-eighth of July they added four new channels to their agent platform. S M S, Telegram, Intercom and Freshdesk. That brings it to nine channels total, alongside phone, web, WhatsApp, Slack and Zendesk.

Here is why that matters and it is not the channel count. You configure the agent once. One set of knowledge, one set of procedures, one set of guardrails. And then the same agent answers a phone call, a WhatsApp message and a support ticket with the same information. You are not maintaining three separate bots that slowly drift apart from each other.

You can also tune behaviour per channel. Terse on S M S, more formal on a support ticket. And you can run simulations against evaluation criteria before you put it live, which matters a great deal when the thing is about to talk to people who pay you.

Now the honest part. Telegram, Intercom and Freshdesk are labelled Alpha by ElevenLabs themselves. That is their word, not mine. The free tier is fifteen minutes a month, which is a prototype budget, not a reception desk. And concurrency is capped low on the cheap plans, four calls at once on free, six on Starter.

Pricing. Six dollars a month for Starter, which is about ninety-nine rand, and that gets you seventy-five minutes. Creator is eleven dollars, roughly a hundred and eighty. Pro jumps to ninety-nine dollars.

Who is this for. If you run a salon, a clinic, a consultancy, anything driven by appointments, this is the one. No-show chasing over WhatsApp and S M S, off one agent, that also picks up the phone after hours. If you are in immigration services, first-line intake and document chasing is exactly the kind of tedious work this handles well.

## SEGMENT 4 - MODEL COUNCIL
### 3:20 to 4:40

**NARRATION:**
Number two. Model Council, now inside Perplexity Computer.

The idea is that you assemble a council of anywhere from two to eight different AI models, give them all the same prompt, let each one work it independently, and then a chair model synthesises where they agree and where they pull apart.

What changed on the twenty-eighth of July is access. This moved into Computer and dropped down to the Individual Pro tier. The capability itself launched back in February.

Where this earns its keep is judgement calls, not lookups. Legal questions. Financial trade-offs. Pricing decisions. Risk. The value is not the consensus, it is the disagreement. Where four models line up and one does not, that is where you need to go and look properly.

I will give you a concrete South African use. You are stress-testing a POPIA compliance decision. Run it past the council. Where the models diverge is precisely where you spend money on an attorney, instead of paying one to confirm the parts you already had right.

The catch is cost, and it is a real catch. Computer bills on usage, a hundred credits to the dollar, and Perplexity did not publish how many credits a Council session burns. A heavy task with generated assets is quoted at up to two thousand two hundred and seventy-five credits. That is about twenty-two dollars, call it three hundred and seventy-five rand, for one run. Individual Pro is twenty dollars a month, roughly three hundred and thirty rand, and comes with four thousand bonus credits, but no stated recurring monthly allowance.

Go in with your eyes open on that one.

## SEGMENT 5 - GOOGLE WORKSPACE DROP
### 4:40 to 6:00

**NARRATION:**
Number three. The Google Workspace July drop, which landed on the twenty-ninth.

The headline item is deck generation in Slides, and this one is better than it sounds. You describe the deck you want, and critically, you point it at an existing deck so it picks up your brand formatting. It then pulls context from your Docs, your Sheets, your PDFs and your previous decks, asks you clarifying questions, and produces a deck you can actually edit.

If you run an agency and you build a monthly report deck from a brief and a spreadsheet, that is your Monday morning back.

In Docs it now synthesises comment threads, drafts replies and suggests edits you accept in one click. It also generates images, diagrams and infographics inline. And in Vids you get auto-storyboarding, auto-scripting, an avatar presenter and voiceover in eight languages.

Three honest problems. Google did not say which plans get what, and their own pricing page shows Gemini in Docs, Sheets and Slides is not supported on Starter, so you realistically need Standard. That is fourteen dollars per user per month, about two hundred and thirty rand, rather than the seven dollars you might have budgeted.

Second, there is no African language support. The eleven languages they added to Docs are Mandarin, Dutch, Malay, Hebrew, Polish, Turkish, Czech, Indonesian, Swedish, Danish and Norwegian. No isiZulu, no Afrikaans in that list, nothing local.

Third, and this one matters if you are writing a POPIA data-flow register. The data-region controls are European Union or United States only. There is no African data region.

## SEGMENT 6 - THE PRICE CUTS
### 6:00 to 8:00

**NARRATION:**
Number four, and this is the one that actually matters. OpenAI cut prices on the thirtieth of July.

GPT five point six Luna came down eighty percent. Terra came down twenty percent.

Here are the numbers. Luna is now twenty cents per million input tokens and a dollar twenty per million output. In rand, that is about three rand thirty and nineteen rand eighty. Terra sits at two dollars and twelve dollars.

Let that sit for a second. Three rand thirty per million input tokens.

If you shelved a feature because the unit economics did not clear against a South African price point, against a subscription you can realistically charge at a hundred and ninety-nine rand a month, go back and redo the arithmetic. Document classification. Intake triage. Summarisation. Bulk processing you are still doing by hand because automation cost more than a junior's time. A lot of those flip this week.

There is also a new Fast mode, up to two and a half times faster at double the price with no change to intelligence, and it replaces Priority Processing. Existing priority requests migrate automatically, so you do not need to do anything.

The limits. Sol, the flagship, got no cut at all. And Luna is not on the Free or Go tiers in ChatGPT Work and Codex, those get Terra only.

And then, number five, DeepSeek went and undercut all of it.

On the thirty-first they promoted V four Flash out of preview. Two hundred and eighty-four billion parameters, thirteen billion active. Fourteen cents per million input tokens on a cache miss. Twenty-eight cents output. And on a cache hit, twenty-eight hundredths of a cent, which is about five South African cents.

The smaller build beats their much larger V four Pro preview on all nine agentic benchmarks they publish. And they released MIT-licensed open weights the same day.

That last point is the one to pay attention to if you handle sensitive client data. The hosted API is China-hosted, which for POPIA purposes is a conversation you do not want to be having. But the open weights mean you can run it on your own infrastructure and the question disappears entirely.

Their own changelog still calls the API public beta. Factor that in.

## SEGMENT 8 - NOTION AND NUDGEFORME
### 9:00 to 10:30

**NARRATION:**
Number seven. Notion, thirty-first of July, added a trigger that fires a Custom Agent the moment an AI Meeting Note finishes summarising.

Small feature, big effect. The meeting ends. The tracker updates itself. A recap with decisions and next steps posts to Slack or goes out by email. Feedback becomes tickets. And it fires selectively, so a client call and an internal standup can behave completely differently.

If you are running a consultancy where the recap email is the thing that proves the hour was billable, that is worth real money.

The cost is where it stings. AI Meeting Notes sits on the Business plan at twenty dollars per member per month, about three hundred and thirty rand each. And Custom Agents burn Notion credits on top of the seat, at ten dollars per thousand credits. For a team of five that adds up quicker than you would like.

Number eight. NudgeForMe, which took the top spot on Product Hunt on the first of August.

It reads your sent folder, finds the threads nobody ever replied to, and drafts a follow-up inside your own mailbox. That is the whole product.

It starts in draft mode, so you review, adjust, approve or dismiss before anything goes out, which is the correct default and I am glad they chose it. It works with Gmail, Outlook, and generic I M A P or S M T P. That last one matters more here than people realise, because a lot of South African businesses are still on a local host rather than Google or Microsoft.

Product Hunt lists it as free. It is a launch-week product with no track record, and you are handing a third party read access to your sent mail, so think about that if the mailbox holds client matters.

But if you run an immigration practice and you spend your life chasing applicants for outstanding documents, this is the single most directly useful thing on this list.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Total runtime target:** 12:30 to 13:00
- **No music under the price-cut segment** (6:00 to 8:00). Let the numbers land dry.
- **Light bed elsewhere**, low mix, nothing driving.
- **All on-screen text is ASCII-only.** No em dashes, no smart quotes, no curly apostrophes. Use plain hyphens and straight quotes throughout to avoid encoding corruption in the render pipeline.
- **No emoji anywhere**, in overlays or thumbnails.
- **AI disclosure required on-video.** Persistent lower-third or a title-card disclosure stating the visuals and voice are AI-generated. Also flag it in the platform disclosure field at upload.
- **Currency framing:** always say the USD figure first, then "about R-something". Never present a rand figure as exact.
